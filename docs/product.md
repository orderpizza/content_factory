# Product goal

Build a repeatable, profitable AI-assisted social content operation, starting
with one Instagram account. Expand to differentiated accounts and other platforms
only after production and measurement demonstrate value.

## Development priority

Build and test publishing readiness, delivery recovery, then receipts/insights and
cost feedback with synthetic content first. Real account integration is a later,
separately authorized phase. English content-quality, prompt, visual-design and
paid Gemini calibration work is explicitly deferred until the final phase; retain
the accepted baseline and safety infrastructure. This sequencing does not waive
human approval of exact delivery JPEGs, caption/alt text or destination.
The offline rehearsal is operational with fake providers only; it establishes no
real publishing, model-quality or audience metric result. See the [roadmap](roadmap.md).

## Business hypotheses

A focused audience may return for useful, recognizable content; AI may reduce
production time enough to make consistent publishing economical. Sponsorship,
affiliate offers and owned products are possible revenue models, not validated
revenue streams. The owner selected English Learning on Instagram as the first
pilot; validate its offer and acquisition hypothesis before optimizing volume.
Existing English, AI/Tech and Psychology domains are current
editorial capabilities, not evidence of market demand or three operating accounts.

## MVP measures

Before a pilot, agree a time window, baseline and numeric targets for one account.
No production outcome has been established by the review-only pipeline.

| Outcome | Measure and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Useful content | Human acceptance rate, factual corrections and revision rounds per approved post; reject unsafe or inaccurate output. |
| Repeatable operation | Approved posts delivered per week, time from idea to review/publication, operator minutes per post, failed and uncertain deliveries. |
| Audience response | Platform-reported reach, saves, shares and follows per published post, with collection time and denominator. |
| Unit economics | Model/image spend plus operator time and delivery costs per approved/published post; attributed revenue less operating costs over the pilot. |
| Safe operation | Every publication has explicit authorization and exact reviewed assets; uncertain sends are reconciled before retry. |

Record missing data as unknown, not zero. Compare cohorts with the same observation
window; engagement is not revenue. Continue only when quality, sustainable effort
and an explicit economic hypothesis justify the next investment.

[Architecture](architecture.md) separates current capabilities from the target;
[roadmap](roadmap.md) orders the work needed to test these hypotheses.

## English Learning pilot brief

Serve non-native English speakers worldwide at beginner–intermediate levels.
Use A1, A2 and B1 as practical CEFR planning labels, never assessed certification
or a promise of proficiency gains. A post targets one level and one observable
learning goal; it need not suit every learner. Do not assume a shared first
language, nationality, curriculum, occupation, income or cultural background.

| Planning level | Learner can… | Visible level signal and teaching approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Recognize an everyday object or use one short phrase for a concrete purpose. | State “Beginner · A1” with a plain goal in public copy; familiar words, one meaning, concrete picture and short model dialogue. |
| A2 | Complete a familiar interaction or correct one frequent usage error. | State “Elementary · A2” and the situation; short connected examples, clear speaker turns and one manageable contrast. |
| B1 | Choose an expression with attention to context, register and possible interpretations. | State “Intermediate · B1” and the use goal; contextual contrast, explicit limits, no claim to know a speaker's private intention. |

Candidate lesson families and reusable topic matrix:

| Family | Example target / level | Learner action | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vocabulary and common expressions | “umbrella” / A1; “Excuse me” / A1 | Name and borrow an object; get attention politely. | One target/use per lesson, not a dictionary dump; attention-getting is not every use of “Excuse me”. |
| Daily conversation | “Could you say that again?” / A2 | Ask for repetition and understand a helpful reply. | Plausible everyday interaction, no required workplace context. |
| Common mistakes | “I agree” / A2 | Agree with a suggestion; correct “I am agree”. | Label the error visibly; no shame or first-language stereotype. |
| Pragmatic nuance | “I'll think about it” / B1 | Distinguish possible readings and ask a neutral follow-up. | Context/tone can change interpretation; never “always a refusal”. |

Initial language hypothesis: simple English-first teaching with strong visual
explanation can serve multiple first-language groups. This is unvalidated, not a
permanent ban on localization. Explain unfamiliar teaching words using simpler
English; avoid unexplained idioms, grammar jargon and dense paragraphs. Use
concrete visual referents, clear speaker attribution, readable contrast and
whitespace. Meaning must not depend on color alone, tiny text or culturally
specific gestures. Review alt text and caption for an equivalent useful lesson.
Mark any genuinely regional usage rather than calling one variety universally
correct. Treat natural variants respectfully.

Each carousel needs an identifiable target, plain meaning, natural contextual
example/dialogue and one small practice action with a model answer or usable
pattern. Preserve all qualifications and exact target wording through adaptation
and rendered slides. Public level/goal signals belong in caption or suitable
lesson copy within existing capacities; do not force new chrome or title labels.
The existing 4–6 slide grammar and capacities remain authoritative in
[content production](specs/content-production.md). Avoid padding a simple lesson
or deleting nuance to fit; flag incompatible lessons for narrower planning.

Exclude current-news dependencies, unsupported origins/etymologies, culture-wide
claims, proficiency guarantees, examination certification, accent superiority,
adult/unsafe examples and specialist jargon requiring an occupation. Longer
grammar courses, pronunciation assessment and localization are later hypotheses.
Visual accessibility, appropriateness of the expression-led grammar for A1
vocabulary, and actual comprehension all require observation. Reviewer judgement
is not learner-outcome evidence. Segment quality and later platform response by
level and lesson family rather than reporting only a universal average.

Phase 2A prepares the [human rubric](testing.md#english-pilot-human-review) and
[pilot procedure](../acceptance/english-pilot.md). It establishes no paid-provider
quality, audience demand or learning efficacy result.
